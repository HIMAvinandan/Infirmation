Psychiatric Administration — Protection, Confidentiality, Non-Disclosure and Information Governance Protocol

I am introducing this Administration as the structured professional and administrative setting through which I undertake psychiatric, therapeutic, psychological, clinical, research, custodial and related authorised functions. The Aspirant is received within a protected therapeutic environment in which confidentiality, continuity, orderly administration and the integrity of records are treated as connected responsibilities rather than isolated formalities. This instrument therefore extends the practical commitments expressed in my earlier Confidentiality and Tariff documents: the professional relationship is to be approached deliberately; information is handled according to purpose, necessity and lawful authority; administrative and financial arrangements are kept distinct from unnecessary clinical disclosure; and the conditions of participation, transfer, communication and continuity are made explicit. The Administration is also designed to remain usable as circumstances, technologies, communications systems, records and administrative mechanisms change. Nothing in this introduction or in the instrument is intended to override mandatory law, professional regulation, patient rights or a competent lawful authority.

Article 1 — PURPOSE

ARTICLE 1 — PURPOSE

1.1 This Protocol establishes a comprehensive framework for protection, confidentiality, non-disclosure, controlled disclosure, information governance, record custody, security, continuity, notification, relocation, financial administration and lawful future administration.

1.2 It applies to information and administrative circumstances existing at adoption and, insofar as lawfully permissible, to subsequent technologies, methods, persons, locations, institutions, financial mechanisms, communications systems, records and circumstances.

1.3 The Protocol shall be interpreted to preserve confidentiality and security while permitting disclosures, access and processing that are legally required, clinically necessary, expressly authorised or otherwise lawfully permitted.

Article 2 — DEFINITIONS

ARTICLE 2 — DEFINITIONS

2.1 “Psychiatric Administration” means the administrative, clinical, therapeutic, psychological, professional, informational, financial, technological, evidentiary and related activity governed by this Protocol.

2.2 “Protected Information” includes personal, clinical, psychiatric, psychological, therapeutic, administrative, financial, technical, security, proprietary, research, evidentiary and derived information, whether recorded physically, electronically, verbally, visually, computationally or by any future means.

2.3 “Administrator” means the Sole Proprietor and Administrator and any person expressly delegated a function under this Protocol.

2.4 “Authorised Person” means a person whose access, use or receipt of Protected Information is authorised for a defined lawful purpose.

2.5 “Disclosure” includes communication, publication, transmission, display, access, retrieval, release, copying, reproduction, export or otherwise making information available to another person or system.

2.6 “Information System” includes present and future electronic, digital, computational, automated, artificial-intelligence, machine-learning, cloud, networked, cryptographic, biometric, distributed or successor technologies used to create, process, store, transmit or analyse information.

2.7 “Record” includes an original, copy, derivative, metadata, log, annotation, correspondence, deposition, retrieval, publication, archive or other evidentiary or administrative representation.

Article 3 — CONFIDENTIALITY

ARTICLE 3 — CONFIDENTIALITY

3.1 Protected Information shall be treated as confidential except to the extent disclosure is authorised, necessary, legally required or otherwise lawfully permitted.

3.2 Access shall ordinarily be limited according to purpose, necessity and lawful authority.

3.3 No person shall use Protected Information for an unauthorised purpose merely because the person has obtained lawful or accidental access to it.

3.4 Confidentiality continues after cessation of treatment, employment, engagement, access, residence, administration or other relationship, subject to applicable law.

Article 4 — THERAPEUTIC AND CLINICAL CONFIDENTIALITY

ARTICLE 4 — THERAPEUTIC AND CLINICAL CONFIDENTIALITY

4.1 Communications and information arising in psychiatric, psychological, therapeutic or related professional activity shall receive the highest protection lawfully available.

4.2 Clinical information shall not be disclosed beyond what is reasonably necessary for the lawful and legitimate purpose of the disclosure.

4.3 Nothing in this Protocol creates an absolute prohibition against disclosures that applicable law requires or permits in circumstances including judicial process, statutory duties, serious safety concerns or other legally recognised exceptions.

Article 5 — NON-DISCLOSURE AND NON-USE

ARTICLE 5 — NON-DISCLOSURE AND NON-USE

5.1 Protected Information shall not be copied, recorded, transmitted, published, sold, exploited, incorporated into an unrelated system or otherwise used without lawful authority.

5.2 No confidentiality obligation shall be defeated merely by changing the medium, format, location, terminology or technological method by which information is represented.

5.3 Information derived from Protected Information remains protected to the extent that it permits identification, reconstruction or materially reveals protected substance.

Article 6 — CONTROLLED DISCLOSURE

ARTICLE 6 — CONTROLLED DISCLOSURE

6.1 Disclosure shall, wherever practicable, be:

(a) for a defined purpose;

(b) limited to the minimum necessary information;

(c) made to an appropriate recipient;

(d) recorded where material; and

(e) undertaken through a reasonably secure method.

6.2 Permitted categories may include patient-authorised disclosure, professional communication, administrative necessity, emergency circumstances, lawful judicial or regulatory requirement, financial/accounting necessity and other lawful purposes.

6.3 A disclosure shall not be treated as a general waiver of confidentiality.

ARTICLE 7 — INFORMATION LIFECYCLE

Article 8 — RECORDS, CUSTODY AND PROVENANCE

ARTICLE 8 — RECORDS, CUSTODY AND PROVENANCE

8.1 Records shall be maintained with reasonable regard to authenticity, integrity, provenance, continuity and accessibility.

8.2 A later copy, retrieval, publication or administrative representation shall not automatically replace or extinguish the evidentiary status of an earlier record.

8.3 Where a record identifies an external publication or resource, the reference may establish location and informational correspondence at the relevant time without necessarily constituting the entire substance, identity or subsequent development of that resource.

8.4 Amendments, annotations and corrections should, where material, preserve an adequate record of the earlier state rather than silently obscuring it.

Article 9 — INTER-SYSTEM COMPATIBILITY AND NON-PREJUDICE

ARTICLE 9 — INTER-SYSTEM COMPATIBILITY AND NON-PREJUDICE

9.1 The Psychiatric Administration may refer to, interface with, retrieve from, preserve, document or otherwise make lawful informational use of associated resources including, as applicable, The IU Ordinal (IUO), The IU Technology (IUT), The Universal Parent Context (IU-UPC), The Ordinal Intelligence, ODS, Deposition (TrT), and associated or successor resources.

9.2 Such references are complementary and informational unless an express instrument provides otherwise.

9.3 A reference shall not, by itself, constitute adoption, incorporation, merger, assignment, transfer, licensing, delegation, endorsement, waiver, relinquishment or alteration of independent ownership, authorship, provenance, intellectual property, evidentiary status, governance or administrative authority.

9.4 The existence of this Protocol shall not prejudice any lawful interest, record, system, methodology or resource existing independently of the Psychiatric Administration.

9.5 Conversely, reference to an external or associated resource shall not be construed as exempting activity conducted within the Psychiatric Administration from the protections and requirements of this Protocol.

ARTICLE 10 — PRESENT PUBLICATION AND LOCATIONAL EVIDENCE

10.1 The publication serving as the primary enumeration of resources at the relevant time may be treated as a locational evidentiary reference to the informational reciprocation and resource enumeration represented at the time of publication.

10.2 Such publication is not necessarily exhaustive of the underlying resources and shall not prevent subsequent correction, extension, refinement, improvement or supersession.

Article 11 — TECHNOLOGICAL NEUTRALITY AND FUTURE APPLICATION

ARTICLE 11 — TECHNOLOGICAL NEUTRALITY AND FUTURE APPLICATION

11.1 This Protocol applies, insofar as legally permissible, to future technologies, information formats, communications methods, computational systems, artificial intelligence, automated processing, financial technologies and successor mechanisms.

11.2 The absence of an expressly enumerated future circumstance shall not itself constitute permission to disclose, access, use, transfer or exploit Protected Information.

Article 12 — NOTIFICATION

ARTICLE 12 — NOTIFICATION

12.1 Notification may be required for material changes, security incidents, breaches, changes of custody, relocation, interruption of service, financial events, regulatory events or other circumstances materially affecting protected interests.

12.2 Where notification is required, the Administration should identify:

(a) the event;

(b) affected information or function;

(c) persons or entities requiring notification;

(d) timing;

(e) method;

(f) protective measures; and

(g) subsequent corrective action.

12.3 Emergency circumstances may justify immediate protective action before complete notification, provided that subsequent documentation and notification are undertaken where lawfully required.

Article 13 — RELOCATION AND TRANSFER

ARTICLE 13 — RELOCATION AND TRANSFER

13.1 Relocation of the Administration, its personnel, records, technology, financial infrastructure or information-storage environment shall not, merely by reason of relocation, terminate or diminish an existing confidentiality obligation.

13.2 Before or during material relocation, reasonable measures should be taken to protect custody, integrity, continuity and access.

13.3 Transfer of records shall be made only to an authorised or lawfully competent recipient.

13.4 Change of physical or digital location shall not, without more, constitute transfer of ownership or provenance.

Article 14 — FINANCIAL INFORMATION GOVERNANCE

ARTICLE 14 — FINANCIAL INFORMATION GOVERNANCE

14.1 Fees, tariffs, invoices, receipts, payment records, refunds, deposits, banking information, payment-processing information, accounting records and financial correspondence may constitute Protected Information.

14.2 Financial administration shall be separated from unnecessary disclosure of clinical information.

14.3 Lawful accounting, taxation, audit, payment-processing, insurance, regulatory or judicial requirements may constitute permitted disclosure purposes to the extent applicable.

14.4 Financial records shall remain subject to appropriate confidentiality and retention controls.

Article 15 — SECURITY AND INCIDENT RESPONSE

ARTICLE 15 — SECURITY AND INCIDENT RESPONSE

15.1 Reasonable technical, administrative and physical safeguards shall be maintained according to the sensitivity of information and the circumstances.

15.2 Suspected incidents should be addressed through:

Detection → Preservation → Containment → Assessment → Classification → Notification → Remediation → Documentation → Review.

15.3 An accidental disclosure shall be treated as an incident requiring appropriate assessment even where malicious intent is absent.

Article 16 — RESEARCH, EDUCATION, PUBLICATION AND AI

ARTICLE 16 — RESEARCH, EDUCATION, PUBLICATION AND AI

16.1 Research, teaching, publication, case presentation, statistical analysis and educational use shall be undertaken only with appropriate authority and safeguards.

16.2 De-identification shall not be assumed merely because names are removed where other information could reasonably permit identification.

16.3 Artificial-intelligence or automated processing of Protected Information shall remain subject to the same confidentiality, purpose, security and lawful-authority requirements applicable to other processing.

Article 18 — AUTHORITY, DELEGATION AND CONTINUITY

ARTICLE 18 — AUTHORITY, DELEGATION AND CONTINUITY

18.1 The Sole Proprietor and Administrator retains the governing administrative authority except where authority is expressly delegated, legally displaced or otherwise constrained by applicable law.

18.2 Delegation of a function does not necessarily constitute transfer of underlying ownership or ultimate administrative responsibility.

18.3 Temporary incapacity, absence, relocation, succession, suspension or cessation shall be administered so as to preserve continuity, security and lawful custody.

Article 19 — RISK AND CONTINGENCY CLASSIFICATION

ARTICLE 19 — RISK AND CONTINGENCY CLASSIFICATION

19.1 Circumstances may be classified according to risk and consequence:

P0 — Routine;

P1 — Foreseeable;

P2 — Material;

P3 — Serious;

P4 — Critical/Emergency;

Article 24 — INTERPRETIVE PRINCIPLE

ARTICLE 24 — INTERPRETIVE PRINCIPLE

This Protocol shall be interpreted to preserve, to the maximum extent lawfully possible:

(a) patient and therapeutic confidentiality;

(b) professional integrity;

(c) administrative continuity;

(d) information security;

(e) record integrity and provenance;

(f) lawful financial administration;

(g) technological neutrality;

(h) compatibility with associated resources;

(i) independent rights and interests; and

(j) lawful future development.

Nothing herein shall be interpreted as creating exceptional treatment merely because the same Sole Proprietor and Administrator performs multiple professional, administrative, intellectual or institutional functions. Distinctions shall be made only where required for a legitimate legal, professional, clinical, financial, evidentiary or administrative purpose.
