REVISED INTRODUCTION
I am introducing this Administration as the structured professional and administrative setting through which I undertake psychiatric, therapeutic, psychological, clinical, research, custodial and related authorised functions. The Aspirant is received within a protected therapeutic environment in which confidentiality, continuity, orderly administration and the integrity of records are treated as connected responsibilities rather than isolated formalities. This instrument therefore extends the practical commitments expressed in my earlier Confidentiality and Tariff documents: the professional relationship is to be approached deliberately; information is handled according to purpose, necessity and lawful authority; administrative and financial arrangements are kept distinct from unnecessary clinical disclosure; and the conditions of participation, transfer, communication and continuity are made explicit. The Administration is also designed to remain usable as circumstances, technologies, communications systems, records and administrative mechanisms change. Nothing in this introduction or in the instrument is intended to override mandatory law, professional regulation, patient rights or a competent lawful authority.

PREAMBLE

This Protocol establishes the protective, confidential, non-disclosure, information-governance, administrative, clinical, financial, technological, evidentiary and continuity principles applicable to the Psychiatric Administration.

The Psychiatric Administration is administered by its Sole Proprietor and Administrator, who may also act, within the scope lawfully applicable to the relevant activity, as psychiatrist, therapist, psychologist, clinician, researcher, author, custodian, administrator and other authorised professional or institutional functionary.

The several capacities of the Sole Proprietor and Administrator are recognised as functions of one administration and are not, merely by reason of their differing descriptions, to be construed as creating artificial or exceptional separation between them. Where a distinction is legally, clinically, professionally, financially or administratively necessary, the distinction shall be made for that particular purpose without creating an unnecessary alteration of the underlying administrative identity.

The designation of the Sole Proprietor and Administrator as having Ordinal Superiority, and the existence of associated Ordinal, technological, informational, evidentiary or administrative resources, shall be recognised as part of the informational context in which this Administration operates. Such recognition shall not, by itself, transfer ownership, alter provenance, create a licence, constitute incorporation, or extinguish any independent right, interest, record or governance status.

ARTICLE 1 — PURPOSE

1.1 This Protocol establishes a comprehensive framework for protection, confidentiality, non-disclosure, controlled disclosure, information governance, record custody, security, continuity, notification, relocation, financial administration and lawful future administration.

1.2 It applies to information and administrative circumstances existing at adoption and, insofar as lawfully permissible, to subsequent technologies, methods, persons, locations, institutions, financial mechanisms, communications systems, records and circumstances.

1.3 The Protocol shall be interpreted to preserve confidentiality and security while permitting disclosures, access and processing that are legally required, clinically necessary, expressly authorised or otherwise lawfully permitted.

ARTICLE 2 — DEFINITIONS

2.1 “Psychiatric Administration” means the administrative, clinical, therapeutic, psychological, professional, informational, financial, technological, evidentiary and related activity governed by this Protocol.

2.2 “Protected Information” includes personal, clinical, psychiatric, psychological, therapeutic, administrative, financial, technical, security, proprietary, research, evidentiary and derived information, whether recorded physically, electronically, verbally, visually, computationally or by any future means.

2.3 “Administrator” means the Sole Proprietor and Administrator and any person expressly delegated a function under this Protocol.

2.4 “Authorised Person” means a person whose access, use or receipt of Protected Information is authorised for a defined lawful purpose.

2.5 “Disclosure” includes communication, publication, transmission, display, access, retrieval, release, copying, reproduction, export or otherwise making information available to another person or system.

2.6 “Information System” includes present and future electronic, digital, computational, automated, artificial-intelligence, machine-learning, cloud, networked, cryptographic, biometric, distributed or successor technologies used to create, process, store, transmit or analyse information.

2.7 “Record” includes an original, copy, derivative, metadata, log, annotation, correspondence, deposition, retrieval, publication, archive or other evidentiary or administrative representation.

ARTICLE 3 — CONFIDENTIALITY

3.1 Protected Information shall be treated as confidential except to the extent disclosure is authorised, necessary, legally required or otherwise lawfully permitted.

3.2 Access shall ordinarily be limited according to purpose, necessity and lawful authority.

3.3 No person shall use Protected Information for an unauthorised purpose merely because the person has obtained lawful or accidental access to it.

3.4 Confidentiality continues after cessation of treatment, employment, engagement, access, residence, administration or other relationship, subject to applicable law.

ARTICLE 4 — THERAPEUTIC AND CLINICAL CONFIDENTIALITY

4.1 Communications and information arising in psychiatric, psychological, therapeutic or related professional activity shall receive the highest protection lawfully available.

4.2 Clinical information shall not be disclosed beyond what is reasonably necessary for the lawful and legitimate purpose of the disclosure.

4.3 Nothing in this Protocol creates an absolute prohibition against disclosures that applicable law requires or permits in circumstances including judicial process, statutory duties, serious safety concerns or other legally recognised exceptions.

ARTICLE 5 — NON-DISCLOSURE AND NON-USE

5.1 Protected Information shall not be copied, recorded, transmitted, published, sold, exploited, incorporated into an unrelated system or otherwise used without lawful authority.

5.2 No confidentiality obligation shall be defeated merely by changing the medium, format, location, terminology or technological method by which information is represented.

5.3 Information derived from Protected Information remains protected to the extent that it permits identification, reconstruction or materially reveals protected substance.

ARTICLE 6 — CONTROLLED DISCLOSURE

6.1 Disclosure shall, wherever practicable, be:
(a) for a defined purpose;
(b) limited to the minimum necessary information;
(c) made to an appropriate recipient;
(d) recorded where material; and
(e) undertaken through a reasonably secure method.

6.2 Permitted categories may include patient-authorised disclosure, professional communication, administrative necessity, emergency circumstances, lawful judicial or regulatory requirement, financial/accounting necessity and other lawful purposes.

6.3 A disclosure shall not be treated as a general waiver of confidentiality.

ARTICLE 7 — INFORMATION LIFECYCLE

Protected Information shall be governed through its lifecycle:

Creation → Collection → Classification → Access → Use → Disclosure → Transmission → Storage → Modification → Archival → Transfer → Retention → Lawful Destruction.

Controls may be applied proportionately at every stage.

ARTICLE 8 — RECORDS, CUSTODY AND PROVENANCE

8.1 Records shall be maintained with reasonable regard to authenticity, integrity, provenance, continuity and accessibility.

8.2 A later copy, retrieval, publication or administrative representation shall not automatically replace or extinguish the evidentiary status of an earlier record.

8.3 Where a record identifies an external publication or resource, the reference may establish location and informational correspondence at the relevant time without necessarily constituting the entire substance, identity or subsequent development of that resource.

8.4 Amendments, annotations and corrections should, where material, preserve an adequate record of the earlier state rather than silently obscuring it.

ARTICLE 9 — INTER-SYSTEM COMPATIBILITY AND NON-PREJUDICE

9.1 The Psychiatric Administration may refer to, interface with, retrieve from, preserve, document or otherwise make lawful informational use of associated resources including, as applicable, The IU Ordinal (IUO), The IU Technology (IUT), The Universal Parent Context (IU-UPC), The Ordinal Intelligence, ODS, Deposition (TrT), and associated or successor resources.

9.2 Such references are complementary and informational unless an express instrument provides otherwise.

9.3 A reference shall not, by itself, constitute adoption, incorporation, merger, assignment, transfer, licensing, delegation, endorsement, waiver, relinquishment or alteration of independent ownership, authorship, provenance, intellectual property, evidentiary status, governance or administrative authority.

9.4 The existence of this Protocol shall not prejudice any lawful interest, record, system, methodology or resource existing independently of the Psychiatric Administration.

9.5 Conversely, reference to an external or associated resource shall not be construed as exempting activity conducted within the Psychiatric Administration from the protections and requirements of this Protocol.

ARTICLE 10 — PRESENT PUBLICATION AND LOCATIONAL EVIDENCE

10.1 The publication serving as the primary enumeration of resources at the relevant time may be treated as a locational evidentiary reference to the informational reciprocation and resource enumeration represented at the time of publication.

10.2 Such publication is not necessarily exhaustive of the underlying resources and shall not prevent subsequent correction, extension, refinement, improvement or supersession.

10.3 The primary enumeration presently identified includes:
(a) IUO — The IU Ordinal;
(b) IUT — The IU Technology; and
(c) IU-UPC — The Universal Parent Context.

10.4 The character of a publication as locational evidence shall be distinguished from the complete substantive identity or future development of the referenced resource.

ARTICLE 11 — TECHNOLOGICAL NEUTRALITY AND FUTURE APPLICATION

11.1 This Protocol applies, insofar as legally permissible, to future technologies, information formats, communications methods, computational systems, artificial intelligence, automated processing, financial technologies and successor mechanisms.

11.2 The absence of an expressly enumerated future circumstance shall not itself constitute permission to disclose, access, use, transfer or exploit Protected Information.

11.3 Future application shall remain subject to mandatory applicable law and professional obligations.

ARTICLE 12 — NOTIFICATION

12.1 Notification may be required for material changes, security incidents, breaches, changes of custody, relocation, interruption of service, financial events, regulatory events or other circumstances materially affecting protected interests.

12.2 Where notification is required, the Administration should identify:
(a) the event;
(b) affected information or function;
(c) persons or entities requiring notification;
(d) timing;
(e) method;
(f) protective measures; and
(g) subsequent corrective action.

12.3 Emergency circumstances may justify immediate protective action before complete notification, provided that subsequent documentation and notification are undertaken where lawfully required.

ARTICLE 13 — RELOCATION AND TRANSFER

13.1 Relocation of the Administration, its personnel, records, technology, financial infrastructure or information-storage environment shall not, merely by reason of relocation, terminate or diminish an existing confidentiality obligation.

13.2 Before or during material relocation, reasonable measures should be taken to protect custody, integrity, continuity and access.

13.3 Transfer of records shall be made only to an authorised or lawfully competent recipient.

13.4 Change of physical or digital location shall not, without more, constitute transfer of ownership or provenance.

ARTICLE 14 — FINANCIAL INFORMATION GOVERNANCE

14.1 Fees, tariffs, invoices, receipts, payment records, refunds, deposits, banking information, payment-processing information, accounting records and financial correspondence may constitute Protected Information.

14.2 Financial administration shall be separated from unnecessary disclosure of clinical information.

14.3 Lawful accounting, taxation, audit, payment-processing, insurance, regulatory or judicial requirements may constitute permitted disclosure purposes to the extent applicable.

14.4 Financial records shall remain subject to appropriate confidentiality and retention controls.

ARTICLE 15 — SECURITY AND INCIDENT RESPONSE

15.1 Reasonable technical, administrative and physical safeguards shall be maintained according to the sensitivity of information and the circumstances.

15.2 Suspected incidents should be addressed through:
Detection → Preservation → Containment → Assessment → Classification → Notification → Remediation → Documentation → Review.

15.3 An accidental disclosure shall be treated as an incident requiring appropriate assessment even where malicious intent is absent.

ARTICLE 16 — RESEARCH, EDUCATION, PUBLICATION AND AI

16.1 Research, teaching, publication, case presentation, statistical analysis and educational use shall be undertaken only with appropriate authority and safeguards.

16.2 De-identification shall not be assumed merely because names are removed where other information could reasonably permit identification.

16.3 Artificial-intelligence or automated processing of Protected Information shall remain subject to the same confidentiality, purpose, security and lawful-authority requirements applicable to other processing.

ARTICLE 17 — PROPRIETARY AND ADMINISTRATIVE MATERIAL

17.1 Administrative forms, protocols, terminology, tariff structures, methodologies, software, research, systems, designs and other proprietary material may receive protection independently of patient confidentiality.

17.2 Patient confidentiality and proprietary confidentiality are distinct protections and shall not be interpreted as mutually exclusive.

ARTICLE 18 — AUTHORITY, DELEGATION AND CONTINUITY

18.1 The Sole Proprietor and Administrator retains the governing administrative authority except where authority is expressly delegated, legally displaced or otherwise constrained by applicable law.

18.2 Delegation of a function does not necessarily constitute transfer of underlying ownership or ultimate administrative responsibility.

18.3 Temporary incapacity, absence, relocation, succession, suspension or cessation shall be administered so as to preserve continuity, security and lawful custody.

ARTICLE 19 — RISK AND CONTINGENCY CLASSIFICATION

19.1 Circumstances may be classified according to risk and consequence:
P0 — Routine;
P1 — Foreseeable;
P2 — Material;
P3 — Serious;
P4 — Critical/Emergency;
P5 — Catastrophic/Continuity.

19.2 Classification guides the proportionality and urgency of protective measures and does not itself create legal authority for disclosure.

ARTICLE 20 — GOVERNANCE REVIEW AND AMENDMENT

20.1 This Protocol may be reviewed and amended to reflect legal, professional, technological, financial, administrative or institutional development.

20.2 Material amendments should identify their effective date and preserve adequate version history.

20.3 An amendment shall not retrospectively authorise conduct that was prohibited at the time of occurrence except to the extent expressly permitted by applicable law.

ARTICLE 21 — SEVERABILITY AND LEGAL PRECEDENCE

21.1 If a provision is invalid or unenforceable in a particular jurisdiction or circumstance, the remaining provisions shall continue to the extent lawfully possible.

21.2 Nothing in this Protocol is intended to exclude or override mandatory law, lawful judicial authority, professional obligations or legally protected rights.

21.3 Where provisions conflict with mandatory applicable law, the mandatory law prevails to the extent of the conflict.

ARTICLE 22 — CONTINUING EFFECT

The protective, confidentiality, non-disclosure, provenance, records, security, financial confidentiality and proprietary provisions intended by their nature to survive shall continue after termination, relocation, transfer, cessation of treatment, cessation of administration or technological change, subject to applicable law.

ARTICLE 23 — GENERAL FUTURE-CIRCUMSTANCE PROVISION

The Protocol shall apply, insofar as legally permissible, to circumstances subsequently arising in connection with the Psychiatric Administration, including new persons, technologies, information forms, communications systems, financial mechanisms, locations, institutional arrangements, risks, events and administrative processes, whether or not individually foreseeable at adoption.

The absence of a specific clause shall not be interpreted as an affirmative permission to compromise confidentiality, security, provenance, lawful custody or protected interests.

ARTICLE 24 — INTERPRETIVE PRINCIPLE

This Protocol shall be interpreted to preserve, to the maximum extent lawfully possible:
(a) patient and therapeutic confidentiality;
(b) professional integrity;
(c) administrative continuity;
(d) information security;
(e) record integrity and provenance;
(f) lawful financial administration;
(g) technological neutrality;
(h) compatibility with associated resources;
(i) independent rights and interests; and
(j) lawful future development.

Nothing herein shall be interpreted as creating exceptional treatment merely because the same Sole Proprietor and Administrator performs multiple professional, administrative, intellectual or institutional functions. Distinctions shall be made only where required for a legitimate legal, professional, clinical, financial, evidentiary or administrative purpose.

INTEGRATED ELABORATION — ADMINISTRATION, SIGN-UP, INTERTWINEMENT, DETERIORATIVITY, AI AND COORDINATED ENFORCEMENT

A. GOVERNING ADMINISTRATION AND FUNCTIONAL CAPACITY
A.1 The Sole Proprietor and Administrator may, within the scope lawfully applicable to the relevant activity, perform psychiatric, therapeutic, psychological, clinical, research, authorship, custodial, administrative and other authorised functions of the Psychiatric Administration.
A.2 Differing descriptions of those functions do not, merely by themselves, create artificial separation of the underlying Administration. Separation shall be made where legally, clinically, professionally, financially, evidentially or administratively necessary.
A.3 The Administration shall preserve the distinction between function, authority, evidence, disclosure and liability.

B. ASPIRANT AND PROTECTED THERAPEUTIC ENVIRONMENT
B.1 “Aspirant” is the patient designation used throughout this instrument for the person seeking, entering, receiving, continuing or transferring into the psychiatric, psychological or therapeutic programme.
B.2 The protected therapeutic environment includes the physical, digital, interpersonal, informational, procedural and communicative conditions reasonably necessary for safe, confidential, effective and continuous therapeutic work.
B.3 Protection extends, where reasonably identifying or materially revealing, to identity, existence of the professional relationship, communications, scheduling, records, metadata and associated administrative circumstances.

C. INTERTWINEMENT
C.1 “Intertwinement” means external participation, intervention, access, influence, interference, omission, circumstance or other involvement materially connected to the Aspirant, Administrator, session, practice, communication, records, therapeutic environment or information environment without a defined legitimate purpose, lawful authority, necessity, proportionality, consent, lawful delegation, emergency protective necessity or other lawful basis.
C.2 Intertwinement may be direct or indirect, singular or repeated, physical, verbal, documentary, digital, computational, financial, institutional or otherwise mediated.
C.3 Mere proximity, association or awareness does not by itself establish impermissible intertwinement; authority, purpose, necessity, proportionality, effect and context shall be assessed.
C.4 Technical access or capability does not itself establish lawful authority to access, observe, classify, retrieve or influence protected information.

D. DETERIORATIVITY
D.1 “Deteriorativity” means conduct, omission, circumstance, intervention or condition that causes or materially increases a foreseeable risk of impairment to therapeutic progress, confidentiality, safety, dignity, continuity of care, protected functioning or orderly functioning of the Administration.
D.2 Deteriorativity may be assessed through nature, extent, persistence, severity, connectedness and foreseeable effect.
D.3 Greater or more persistent impermissible intertwinement may correspond to greater deteriorativity. This relationship is evidentiary and contextual; no legal liability is created merely by counting incidents.
D.4 Deteriorativity is not itself a finding of criminality, mens rea, fault or civil liability. Applicable legal and evidentiary standards remain controlling.

E. MINIMUM NECESSARY INVOLVEMENT
E.1 The minimum necessary involvement principle requires the least external access, information, participation, intervention or disclosure reasonably sufficient for the authorised purpose.
E.2 Necessity shall not be presumed merely from convenience, curiosity, technical capability, institutional affiliation or general interest.
E.3 Where a narrower lawful measure can accomplish the protective or administrative purpose, the narrower measure should be preferred.

F. MUTUALISATION AND PROCEDURAL SAFEGUARDS
F.1 “Mutualisation” means human discussion and, where legally and practically available, agreement or coordinated understanding before material action.
F.2 Mutualisation is a procedural safeguard and is not a veto over mandatory law, urgent protective duties or a competent lawful order.
F.3 Where discretionary escalation or disclosure is contemplated and delay is lawful, the Administrator should use the maximum lawful procedural interval for clarification, discussion, review, alternatives and proportionate resolution.
F.4 Silence, non-objection, temporary unawareness, non-discovery or lack of response do not by themselves constitute consent, authorisation, waiver, approval or acquiescence to external involvement.

G. SIGN-UP AND TRANSFER
G.1 The designated reply mechanism is the operative mechanism for completing sign-up into the programme.
G.2 Where the Aspirant is transferring from a previous psychologist, psychiatrist or therapist, the reply may also initiate the coordinated transfer process to the extent authorised.
G.3 Previous professional involvement does not itself create automatic authority for transfer of records or information.
G.4 Transfer remains subject to consent, applicable law, professional requirements, minimum necessary disclosure, continuity of care and secure custody.
G.5 Completion of sign-up establishes participation according to the terms communicated through the sign-up instrument; it does not waive independent statutory or professional rights.

H. ADMINISTRATIVE SUSTENANCE AND REMUNERATION
H.1 Agreed remuneration, tariffs, charges, deposits, invoices or other administrative obligations shall operate according to their stated terms and applicable law.
H.2 Remuneration may support continuity and sustenance of permanent administrative structures, including records, secure systems, professional administration, communications, technological infrastructure and continuity arrangements.
H.3 Non-payment is not automatically criminal and shall not be converted into criminality merely through administrative assertion.
H.4 Lawful recovery or enforcement remains subject to agreement, notice, dispute procedure, proportionality and applicable law.
H.5 Financial information remains subject to appropriate confidentiality and shall not require unnecessary disclosure of clinical information.

I. CRIMINAL NON-IMPUTATION AND ATTRIBUTION
I.1 No person shall be characterised as criminally liable, complicit, conspiratorial or culpable merely through implication, association, proximity, silence, non-objection, temporary unawareness, disagreement, therapeutic circumstance, allegation, administrative irregularity or automated inference without a legally sufficient basis.
I.2 Nothing in this instrument disclaims actual criminal liability where criminal conduct is established under applicable law.
I.3 Allegation does not become finding merely through repetition, AI classification, administrative escalation or third-party participation.
I.4 Enforcement shall not be used to facilitate, induce, manufacture, aggravate or retrospectively construct conduct or implication.
I.5 Where legally permissible, coordinated enforcement should proceed through: established participation/sign-up → event preservation → verification → human review → legal characterisation → authority assessment → necessity and proportionality → minimum necessary intervention → lawful enforcement where required.

J. MANDATORY AUTHORITY AND CONTROLLED DISCLOSURE
J.1 A private protocol cannot defeat a directly applicable mandatory statute or competent lawful order.
J.2 Where disclosure or action is mandatory, the Administrator should, where legally available, verify authority and scope, seek clarification or review, preserve confidentiality, minimise disclosure, document the basis and comply within the lawful scope.
J.3 A disclosure under one lawful purpose is not a general waiver of confidentiality.
J.4 Mandatory authority shall not be treated as broader than the authority actually established.

K. AI, AUTOMATION AND HUMAN CONTROL
K.1 AI may assist with organisation, classification, drafting, retrieval, analysis, workflow, security and other controlled functions.
K.2 AI is augmentative and remains subject to human oversight.
K.3 AI detection is not a finding; a finding is not authorisation; a recommendation is not a decision; and a decision is not a disclosure.
K.4 AI shall not independently disclose Protected Information, notify authorities, initiate legal proceedings, transfer records, grant third-party access, terminate treatment, relocate records or materially alter the protected therapeutic environment.
K.5 AI-generated inference of mens rea is not proof. Mens rea, attribution and legal characterisation require appropriate human and contextual evaluation.
K.6 Automated classification shall not itself create authority, consent, liability or a duty to disclose.

L. ORDINAL, ODS AND ASSOCIATED RESOURCE NON-PREJUDICE
L.1 References to The IU Ordinal (IUO), The IU Technology (IUT), IU-UPC, The Ordinal Intelligence, ODS, Deposition (TrT), ODS-IMPACT and associated resources remain references within their properly defined scope.
L.2 The present publication may serve as a locational evidentiary record of informational reciprocation at publication time without exhausting future development.
L.3 Later correction, extension, refinement, improvement or supersession remains possible.
L.4 Cross-reference does not automatically constitute adoption, incorporation, merger, assignment, transfer, licensing, delegation, endorsement, waiver or alteration of independent ownership, authorship, provenance, intellectual-property, evidentiary, governance or authority status.

M. COORDINATED CONTINUITY AND ENFORCEMENT
M.1 The Administration shall seek to preserve therapeutic continuity, confidentiality, records, security and orderly custody during interruption, relocation, transfer, technological change or dispute.
M.2 Legal, administrative, technological and institutional measures should remain proportionate to the purpose and risk actually established.
M.3 The existence of a dispute, allegation, incident or classification does not itself authorise unlimited access or disclosure.
M.4 The protective purpose of this instrument is to preserve lawful administration rather than to prevent compliance with mandatory law.

N. INTEGRATED INTERPRETIVE PRINCIPLE
N.1 The whole instrument shall be read as one administrative framework: Administration → Authorisation → Necessity → Proportionality → Confidentiality → Minimum Necessary Involvement → Therapeutic Continuity → Documentation → Review.
N.2 No authority + no necessity + no recognised protective purpose = no permissible involvement.
N.3 Where provisions can operate together lawfully, they shall be read cumulatively rather than as implied replacements of the foundational text.
N.4 Where a mandatory rule requires a different result, that mandatory rule controls to the extent of the conflict.
